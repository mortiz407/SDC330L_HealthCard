For my project, I decided to build a console app called Digital Health Card. It’s essentially a pocket medical profile meant for 
students and everyday humans who can never remember the exact spelling of their allergies or what year their last booster shot was
when filling out clinic paperwork. It stores critical details like emergency contacts, allergies, chronic illnesses, and dates of 
birth so first responders or school nurses do not have to guess.


When the app opens, it clears off whatever mess you left in your terminal and hits you with a nice, clean banner. It tells you right 
away that you are looking at the Week 1 prototype of the Health Card system, "Keep your medical card updated." Right below the banner,
there is a one-sentence tip explaining that you just need to type a number to navigate around.


The interface uses a simple numbered menu because medical emergencies are stressful enough without having to guess terminal flags. You
get an easy list press 1 to set up a patient card, 2 to edit a detail, 3 to wipe a record, 4 to print out the whole health card, and 5
to leave. If you pick "Add/Update", the app asks you straightforward questions one by one like your name, date of birth, emergency contact
phone, and your list of allergies and illnesses. If you slip up and smash random keys when it wants a number, the app won't throw a tantrum
or crash; it just asks you to pick a valid number again.


When you hit option 4 to display the card it prints out a clean, formatted medical summary badge. It gives you clear sections for Personal
Details Name, DOB, Emergency Contact information, and an Alert section that flags your known allergies and current illnesses in plain view
so anyone reading it can find what they need instantly. You just enter 5 at the menu prompt. The program drops a friendly farewell like, "Stay
safe, drink some water, and remember to wash your hands!" and exits cleanly.
